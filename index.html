FlowAudioEdit

Training-Free and Inversion-Free Audio Editing with Pre-trained Flow Models

Jiayi Yu · Xiaoran Yang · Gongping Huang

School of Electronic Information, Wuhan University, Wuhan, China

Paper arXiv Code Demo

Abstract

Text-guided audio editing requires modifying content specified by a target prompt while preserving unrelated source information. Existing training-free editing methods can achieve this through source inversion, but reconstructing the source trajectory introduces additional computation and potential reconstruction errors. FlowAudioEdit introduces the inversion-free FlowEdit paradigm into audio editing by adapting FlowEdit to the native latent spaces of two pre-trained text-to-audio flow models, and by performing direct source-to-target editing through the difference between source- and target-conditioned velocity fields.

To account for the different roles of source and target guidance during editing, FlowAudioEdit further introduces dynamic classifier-free guidance, which progressively decreases source guidance while increasing target guidance along the trajectory. Experiments show strong editing and preservation performance on both TangoFlux and Stable Audio 3 backbones without source inversion, while avoiding source inversion reduces computational overhead and enables more efficient inference.

Method

FlowAudioEdit maps source audio into the latent space of a frozen text-to-audio flow model and edits it directly, without reconstructing a source trajectory.

1

FlowEdit for Audio Flow Models

The method adapts FlowEdit to the native latent spaces of TangoFlux and Stable Audio 3 using frozen encoders, decoders, and text-conditioned velocity fields.

2

Direct Source-to-Target Editing

At each active editing step, paired source and target states are constructed with shared noise, and the edited latent is updated by integrating the target-minus-source velocity difference.

3

Progress-Dependent Dynamic CFG

Source guidance gradually decreases while target guidance increases over the active FlowEdit updates, balancing preservation and the desired edit.

Experimental Setup

Paper Benchmark

The paper benchmark is built from AudioCaps clips filtered by audio-text CLAP similarity above 0.4. It contains 3,000 ten-second source-target pairs across addition, removal, and replacement, with 1,000 pairs per task.

Baselines

FlowAudioEdit is compared with MMEdit, AudioEditor, and Stable Audio 3 medium inpainting. The proposed method is evaluated on TangoFlux and Stable Audio 3 medium backbones.

Evaluation

Semantic alignment and preservation are measured with LAION-CLAP metrics. Reference similarity and distance are evaluated with FAD, LSD, LPAPS, MCD, and Mel-L1; efficiency is measured by RTF and peak GPU memory.
